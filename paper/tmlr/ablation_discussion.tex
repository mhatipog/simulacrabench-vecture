Table~\ref{tab:ablation-all} reports every variant on all eleven sets; intervals for the real-survey studies are in Appendix~\ref{app:ci}. We read it in four groups. Refitting every expert with two other seeds on GSS-1 moves the full stack's loss by at most 0.11 milli-nats per cell and leaves the sign of every effect except the (null) empirical-Bayes tables unchanged (Table~\ref{tab:seeds}).

\paragraph{Stacking design.} Stacking itself is the dominant effect: the equal-weight average of the experts loses 6--65 milli-nats per cell, and the single best expert, chosen out-of-fold (LightGBM on GSS and the twin sets, CatBoost on the gate simulators, the network on the UNICEF sandbox), loses 3--23, and 101 on Twin. The exception is Twin-HH, discussed below, where the equal-weight average is 0.4 better than the stack and the best single expert only 0.6 worse (neither interval excludes zero). Among design choices, the gate factorisation is the largest on the real survey (3.7--4.5) and helps on every set. Fitting stacking weights, temperatures and calibration on all out-of-fold rows rather than on a 950-row holdout, the main structural change from our earlier ensemble, is worth 0.9--3.5 on every set (interval excludes zero on ten of eleven). Per-question weights (against pooled weights only) help most where questions differ in which expert is right: 12.6 on Twin, where the neighbour experts dominate some questions, 0.1--1.4 on GSS.

\paragraph{Gate recalibration and temperature.} The isotonic map helps on all three GSS studies (0.4--0.5) and on both twin sets, and is neutral to slightly harmful on the gate simulators (Gate-A $+0.3$); the cross-check keeps it from doing damage where tree gates are already sharp. Temperatures add 0--0.3 on GSS and 1--3 on the twin sets.

\paragraph{Experts.} No direct expert is indispensable everywhere. CatBoost is the only one that matters on all three GSS studies (0.6--1.5); the network and the multinomial logit matter on most synthetic sets (1.0--6.2), which are generated from smooth logits; the empirical-Bayes tables are neutral on GSS and help on the synthetic gate sets. The dedicated gate and answer-if-asked LightGBM models add 0.4--0.7 on GSS.

\paragraph{Neighbour experts and leakage.} The kernel neighbours are the clearest case of an expert whose value depends on a structural property of the data: 82 milli-nats on Twin, where 39\% of rows have a visible cross-round twin, and nothing on GSS, where removing them gains 0.1--0.2 (one interval excludes zero). On Twin-HH, where visible rows have twins in other visible rows but scored rows never do, removing them gains 1.7 (interval includes zero), although the stack puts 0.71 of its direct weight on them. The gap between out-of-fold and held-out skill exposes the mechanism: it is 0.287 against 0.132 on Twin-HH, but 0.265 against 0.287 on Twin and 0.429 against 0.421 on GSS-1. A large positive gap between the stack's out-of-fold skill and its held-out skill is a cheap warning that visible rows share structure that scored rows do not. This is the leakage mechanism of \S\ref{sec:mechanism}, observed here without any change to the folds: the submitted stack uses random folds, and household-grouped folds (H1) were developed but not submitted.
